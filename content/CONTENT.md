### The Story of Sacred Monster

The Cosmic Joke: a stone of more than a thousand pounds that cannot be touched and must be moved.

In 1992 I set out to make a stone sculpture that could not be touched. The inspiration was the Kongo nkisi nkondi, power figures driven with nails. The title was Untouchable You.

Once finished, it became a logistical challenge. It was damaged in 1995 when it was moved from Art City to StoneWorks, Michelle Chapin's studio. In December 1998 a fire at Art City destroyed all my stored work. Safe at StoneWorks, Untouchable You was the sole survivor.

There it sat until 2017, when the sculpture yard was reorganized and I was asked to remove it. With neither the means to move it nor a place to put it, I turned to a client, Norm Fawley, who provided a truck and space at his ranch in Cayucos.

Eight years later, with Norm in failing health, I had to decide whether to finish the piece or abandon it. I repaired the damage, reinstalling the original nails and, unexpectedly, adding many thousands more. Absorbed again in its demands, I let the piece dictate a different path: from untouchability to survivorship, from prohibition to indifference.

Hence the new title: Sacred Monster, or Monstre Sacré. In French culture, a monstre sacré is a figure of such stature that ordinary rules no longer apply. The sculpture had earned the name. It survived what destroyed everything else and requires its own terms, including a hand truck special-ordered for its body: the Dutro Brute, rated at 1,600 lbs, which will accompany Sacred Monster for its lifetime. In June 2026 it was moved without incident by a crew wearing Sacred Monster T-shirts. The move is on video.

*Dedicated to Norm Fawley (1942–2025), a monstre sacré in his own right, without whose generosity it would not have been finished.*
